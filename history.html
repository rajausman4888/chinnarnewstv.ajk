Kashmir History

From Ancient Times to Modern Era

Introduction to Kashmir

Kashmir, often called "Paradise on Earth," has a rich history spanning thousands of years. Nestled between the great Himalayan ranges, this region has been home to diverse civilizations, religions, and cultures. From ancient sages to modern freedom struggles, Kashmir's story is one of resilience and beauty.

Ancient History

The 7.6 magnitude earthquake on October 8, 2005, struck Azad Kashmir and surrounding regions at 8:50 a.m., with its epicenter near Muzaffarabad. It was one of South Asia's worst disasters, causing over 73,000–87,000 deaths, injuring over 38,000, and leaving 3.5 million people homeless. The devastation severely impacted infrastructure, particularly in Muzaffarabad.

Muslim Rule

Azad Jammu and Kashmir (AJK) operates under an Islamic-based legal system where Shari’ah is the supreme law, ensuring no legislation contradicts the Quran and Sunnah. The 1974 Constitution, coupled with the Enforcement of Shari’ah Act 1989, dictates that laws must conform to Islamic teachings, enforced by the AJK Shariat Court and appointed Muftis.

Mughal, Sikh & Dogra Rule

The region now known as Azad Jammu and Kashmir (AJK) was shaped by successive Mughal (1586–1752), Sikh (1819–1846), and Dogra (1846–1947) rules. Mughals treated it as a favored province, Sikhs brought military annexation, and the Dogras established control through the Treaty of Amritsar, influencing local administrative, cultural, and architectural landscapes.

1947 Partition & Modern History

Azad Kashmir was formed following the 1947 Partition of India, when local rebels and Pashtun tribesmen revolted against the Dogra Maharaja Hari Singh, leading to a war between India and Pakistan. Following a 1949 UN-brokered ceasefire, the western part of the state came under Pakistani control, while the region was established as a self-governing entity administered by Pakistan.

Culture & Traditions

Jammu Kashmir – Culture and Tradition | RitiRiwazAzad Kashmir boasts a rich cultural tapestry blending Kashmiri, Pahari, and Punjabi influences, deeply rooted in Islamic traditions. The culture is characterized by vibrant festivals (Eid), traditional attire like Kashmiri shawls and Kurtas, intricate handicrafts (woodwork, embroidery), and aromatic cuisine, including Wazwan and pulao.

Festivals & Tourism

Azad Kashmir is a rapidly growing tourism destination, boasting over 1.5 million visitors in 2025, with peak activities in Neelum Valley, Pir Chinasi, and the Haveli district. The region hosts vibrant cultural festivals featuring local music, traditional dances, and food, alongside tourism-focused events highlighting scenic, high-altitude landscapes

Famous Personalities

Famous personalities of Azad Kashmir (AJK) are largely defined by their roles in the region’s political, historical, and struggle movements. Key figures include founding president Sardar Muhammad Ibrahim Khan, political leaders Chaudhary Ghulam Abbas, Sardar Sikandar Hayat Khan, and Raja Farooq Haider Khan, along with prominent leaders like Masood Khan
